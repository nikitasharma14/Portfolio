## Overview

This project explored the use of device-free sensing technologies, particularly Wi-Fi Channel State Information (CSI), for unobtrusive monitoring of daily activities and behaviour among older adults with cognitive impairment living independently at home. The research addressed this goal through two complementary perspectives: technological development, focusing on Wi-Fi CSI-based human activity recognition and its generalizability in realistic environments; and human-centered investigation, focusing on the needs, expectations, and information requirements of informal caregivers.

## Research Approach & Key Findings

The research was organized into three complementary phases, moving from understanding the state of the art, to developing and testing sensing technologies, and finally to translating sensing information into meaningful caregiver communication.

### 1. Explore & Scope

**Approach:**
A scoping review mapped unobtrusive sensing technologies for human activity recognition and assessed their readiness for implementation in older adult care.

**Key findings:**
Although unobtrusive sensing technologies show strong potential for older adult care, research remains largely technology-driven, with limited attention to **context-aware development and implementation readiness**. Assessing implementation challenges early in technology development can help identify barriers and opportunities for successful adoption.

### 2. Develop & Test

**Approach:**
Wi-Fi CSI was explored for monitoring activities relevant to older adults with cognitive impairment. The **Wi-Gitation dataset** was collected and evaluated in realistic environments, followed by investigations of person- and location-related data mismatch and the development of **InSSeqTra**, a transfer-learning framework for domain adaptation.

**Key findings:**
Wi-Fi CSI shows promise for unobtrusive monitoring, but **real-world performance is strongly affected by person- and location-related data mismatch**. Transfer learning provides an opportunity to improve generalizability while enabling models to adapt to individual characteristics, supporting more personalized monitoring.

### 3. Design to Communicate

**Approach:**
Mixed-method research with informal caregivers and older adults explored the needs, expectations, and information requirements surrounding unobtrusive monitoring. These insights informed the design and evaluation of a low-fidelity caregiver interaction platform incorporating personalization and persuasive system design.

**Key findings:**
Unobtrusive sensing was valued for supporting older adults and caregivers, but successful implementation requires **centralized, empathetic, adaptable, and privacy-conscious care approaches**. Care needs and information requirements change over time, highlighting the importance of adaptable solutions and clear agreements about **when information should be communicated, to whom, and what actions should follow**.


## Implementable Outcomes

The research provides practical insights for developing and implementing unobtrusive sensing solutions in home-based care:

* **Personalized monitoring:** Wi-Fi CSI-based models can be adapted to individual characteristics to improve monitoring in real-world settings.
* **Context-aware development:** Technical performance and implementation challenges should be assessed early and under realistic conditions.
* **Adaptive care support:** Sensing systems should accommodate changing care needs, health conditions, and personal circumstances.
* **Caregiver-centered communication:** Information should be personalized and communicated to the right stakeholder, at the right time, with clear agreements on the required follow-up action.
* **Privacy-conscious design:** Personalization and monitoring should be balanced with privacy, safety, and user expectations.
