## Overview

**MOCIA (Maintaining Optimal Cognitive function In Ageing)** is a multidisciplinary research programme focused on developing personalised lifestyle interventions to support healthy cognitive ageing. The programme brings together research on lifestyle, cognitive functioning, predictive factors, biological mechanisms, behavioural change, and eHealth to develop personalised support for older adults.


## Research Approach & Key Findings

My work was part of **Work Package 4: Personalised e-Health**, which focused on translating knowledge from the wider MOCIA programme into a digital toolkit for at-home use. The work explored how technology can support older adults in maintaining healthy behaviours across multiple lifestyle domains, including physical activity, sleep, nutrition, and cognitive health.

**Design Vision:** The design vision was to develop a personalised, user-centered digital health toolkit that supports older adults in integrating healthy behaviours into their everyday lives. The approach aimed to move beyond one-size-fits-all interventions by considering individual needs, preferences, goals, capabilities, and context.

The research followed the **Double Diamond** design approach, integrating human-centered design and behavioural science to **understand users, define opportunities, develop solutions, and evaluate them with users and stakeholders**. More than **50 older adults and 15 experts** were involved across different stages of the design and development process.


### 1. Discover

**Approach:** Explored the needs, experiences, motivations, expectations, and barriers of older adults participating in multidomain lifestyle interventions, alongside the perspectives of professionals involved in their delivery and development. Four qualitative studies across **FINGER-NL, HELI, and MOCIA** provided insights into engagement, adherence, lifestyle change, and expectations for digital support.

**Key findings:** Older adults differed in their motivations, capabilities, preferences, and engagement across lifestyle domains. Sustained participation was influenced by **personal circumstances, perceived benefits, motivation, existing habits, support, and the flexibility to adapt engagement over time**. These findings highlighted the need for personalised support that fits individual goals and everyday contexts.

### 2. Define

**Approach:** Findings from the Discover phase were translated into a **design vision, behavioural goals, and functional and experiential design features** for the Schouderklopje digital toolkit.

**Key features identified:**

* **Multidomain self-management** across six lifestyle domains
* **Adaptive goal setting** based on individual needs, capabilities, and circumstances
* **Personalised training and feedback** that allows users to choose and adjust the intensity of different domains
* **Progress monitoring and visual progress reports**
* **Education and skill development** through concise, accessible, and engaging content
* **Social connection** with partners, family, or wider social networks where appropriate
* **Pause and restart functionality** to accommodate changing circumstances
* **Praise and encouraging feedback** to support motivation
* **Autonomy, trust, and privacy** as core experiential requirements
* **Credible and transparent information** to support trust in the toolkit

### 3. Develop

**Approach:** The identified requirements were translated into concepts and high-fidelity prototypes for the **Schouderklopje** personalised eHealth toolkit. The multidisciplinary research team iteratively developed and refined the overall toolkit and individual lifestyle-domain components.

**Key outcome:** The development process translated user and expert needs into a coherent set of **personalisation, self-management, education, feedback, social, and flexibility features**, while allowing their implementation to be adapted to the characteristics of individual lifestyle domains.

### 4. Deliver

**Approach:** The developed features were evaluated and refined through multiple feasibility studies involving older adults. The **adaptive goal-setting framework**, for example, was developed and evaluated within the physical activity and sleep domains, while personalised suggestions were explored for diet and nutrition.

**Key outcome:** Iterative evaluation provided insights into the **usability, relevance, acceptability, and practical implementation** of the developed features and informed further refinement of the toolkit. The final design aimed to provide personalised multidomain support while maintaining **user autonomy, flexibility, and engagement**.

### Current Status & Funding

**Schouderklopje** is currently being evaluated in a real-world trial with 60 older adults.

This research was conducted as part of **MOCIA (Maintaining Optimal Cognitive function In Ageing)**, a multidisciplinary research programme funded through the **NWO Cross-over programme**. [Learn more about MOCIA →](https://mocia.nl/)
