## Overview

This project explored how **Digital Nature, a 24-hour streaming virtual nature environment**, could enhance the experience and well-being of patients in the intensive care unit (ICU). A 7-day mixed-methods case study was conducted with 5 ICU patients, combining quantitative measures and semi-structured interviews with patients and ICU nurses.

The study examined patients’ experiences with Digital Nature and nurses’ perspectives on its perceived effects, practical challenges, and potential integration into everyday ICU care. Preliminary findings suggest that Digital Nature has the potential to support relaxation and positive social interactions between patients and nurses. At the same time, responses varied considerably between patients, highlighting the importance of personalised digital nature experiences that can adapt to individual preferences, clinical conditions, and changing needs during recovery.

The study also identified practical considerations for implementing digital nature in intensive care, including the need to account for the clinical context, patient condition, timing, and existing care workflows. Overall, the findings provide initial insights into how digital nature can be used as a **non-pharmacological approach to supporting patient wellbeing and experience in intensive care**.
